If water is flowing, close the main valve, open the lowest tap, lift valuables off the floor and keep power off in wet rooms unless the panel is dry. Frozen pipe water damage is the flooding that follows when ice blocks a line, pressure builds behind it and the pipe wall splits. Water expands by roughly 9 percent as it freezes, and the split is often hidden until the ice melts and flow returns.

Frozen pipe: what to do right now

Speed matters most at the moment of thaw. These steps avoid a second injury, which is usually a fire or a shock.

1Find the main valve

Locate it before the freeze breaks, so you can close it in seconds.

2Open the faucet

A slightly open tap lets thawing water and pressure escape instead of building behind ice.

3Warm the area gently

Use a hair dryer or warm towels on the frozen section, working toward the faucet. Never use a torch or open flame.

4Check for a split

Look for a bulge, a frosty crack or dripping after the pipe warms.

5Stay clear of power

If water is near outlets or the panel, call the utility or 911 before calling us.

6Call dispatch

Give the floor, the rooms wet and whether water is still moving.

Cold snaps that catch La Vista homes off guard

The National Weather Service office in Omaha logged a record low of 21 degrees on April 15, 2020, and another record of 20 degrees on October 26 of that year. Freezes outside the dead of winter catch homeowners after they have stopped paying attention to hose bibs, garages and exterior walls.

Pipes most at risk sit in unheated garages, along exterior walls, in attic spaces and near outdoor spigots. If your home has any of those, shut off and drain outdoor lines early in the fall and check them after a hard frost.

Signs that a pipe has frozen or split

  • A faucet that runs as a trickle or not at all, especially on one side of the house
  • Frost on an exposed pipe or a visible bulge in the line
  • A damp patch on drywall that was dry the day before
  • The sound of running water in a wall when every fixture is off
  • A ceiling stain or drip that appears after a cold night warms up

Any one of these after a freeze is worth a call. A hidden split can run for hours behind a finished wall.

How the damage is assessed and dried

Crews begin with a moisture meter and thermal imaging camera to trace where the thaw water traveled, then extract standing water and open the wall bays that read wet. The goal is to dry framing without tearing out more than is necessary.

Insulation that has taken on water is usually removed, because it holds moisture against studs. Air movers and dehumidifiers then run until readings return to a dry reference, a process described on the structural drying page. If the split sat above a finished ceiling, the ceiling water damage repair page covers that stage.

What drives the cost after a freeze

Frozen pipe losses range from one wet closet to several floors. The scope is written before work starts, so each stage is clear.

  • Number of splits. Several splits along one wall mean more openings and more drying.
  • Time before shutoff. Water that ran overnight reaches farther than water caught in minutes.
  • Insulation and drywall removed. Wet insulation and soft drywall are removed and replaced.
  • Equipment days. Exterior walls in cold weather can dry slowly.
  • Finish repairs. Paint, trim and flooring are separate from mitigation.

Sudden water damage from a pipe that froze and split is often covered by a homeowners policy, but policies can require reasonable steps to prevent freezing. Review your policy or ask your insurer. Thaw-related losses are handled under ANSI/IICRC S500 practice like any other clean water loss, with readings taken until materials are dry.

Frozen pipe questions in La Vista

Do you handle frozen pipe water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in La Vista?

Yes. Dispatch runs 24/7, including holidays and cold nights. Tell the dispatcher whether the pipe has already split and whether water is still flowing.

Is it safe to thaw a pipe myself?

Gentle warmth from a hair dryer or warm towels is generally safe. Never use a torch or open flame, and stop and call if you see a split or water near electrical equipment.

My pipe thawed and nothing leaked. Am I in the clear?

Check the line again after an hour and look at the wall behind it. A small split can drip slowly, and a damp patch is a reason to have moisture readings taken.

How do I keep pipes from freezing again?

Insulate exposed lines, seal gaps where cold air enters, disconnect hoses, keep heat on during cold snaps and open cabinet doors on exterior walls.

Will a homeowners policy pay for a frozen pipe?

Sudden freeze damage is often covered, though terms vary. Ask your insurer what steps the policy expects you to take, and document the damage.
