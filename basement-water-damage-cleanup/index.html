A flooded or seeping basement is an emergency, and La Vista dispatch is open 24/7. Stay off the stairs if water is near the electrical panel, turn off power only from a dry location, move valuables upstairs and keep children and pets away. If water is rising fast, leave the basement and call from upstairs.

Basement water damage is the water, dampness and mold risk in a below-grade room, whether it comes from seepage through walls or floor, a failed sump pump, a window well overflow or a backed-up drain. It differs from a crawl space or main-floor leak because the water often comes from outside and sits against concrete.

When basement water is an emergency

A basement can fill faster than it looks. These steps keep people out of danger.

1Stay out of deep water

Water above the first stair tread or near the panel is not safe to enter.

2Check the sump pump

If the pump is running and water is still rising, or the power is out, call us and your plumber.

3Turn off gas and furnace if needed

If water reaches the furnace or water heater burner, shut them off from a safe location.

4Move belongings up

Lift boxes, rugs and furniture. Pull wet cardboard off the concrete.

5Photograph

Capture the waterline and any visible cracks or window well overflow.

6Call dispatch

Tell us the depth, the source and whether the water smells.

Basements and the Big Papillion Creek basin

La Vista's earliest homes were built in 1959 and 1960 on land overlooking the Big Papillion Creek basin, and the metro's flood story, with the Papillion Creek watershed covering about 400 square miles, shapes how homeowners think about groundwater. Groundwater levels rise after wet periods and push on foundation walls.

Whether your lot is mapped in a floodplain is a question for the FEMA flood map and the Papio-Missouri River Natural Resources District's online viewers. Check the FEMA flood map for your address, and remember that seepage is not the same as creek flooding for insurance.

Restoration is not waterproofing

Cleaning and drying a basement does not stop water from returning. Drainage, sump upgrades, grading and waterproofing are separate projects by other contractors. Ask the City of La Vista Building Department at 402-593-6400 whether structural or drainage work needs a permit.

If water keeps coming back after a clean-up, fix the source before rebuilding finishes. Otherwise new drywall and carpet will go in over the same problem.

What shapes the cost of basement cleanup

Basement jobs depend on depth, finishes and the source. A written scope and price are explained before work starts.

  • Depth and duration. More water that sat longer reaches more materials.
  • Finishes. Finished basements with carpet and drywall cost more than bare concrete.
  • Water type. Clean seepage and sewage-like water are handled differently.
  • Drying days. Concrete and cold air slow drying.
  • Source repair. Sump, drainage and waterproofing are separate.

Basement water damage questions in La Vista

Do you handle basement water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in La Vista?

Yes. Dispatch is open 24/7. Call from upstairs if water is deep or near electrical equipment.

Is basement seepage covered by insurance?

The state insurance department says flooding is not covered under a homeowners policy. Whether seepage is covered depends on your policy wording, so ask your agent.

Will you fix my sump pump?

A plumber or pump contractor repairs or replaces the pump. We remove water and dry the space.

How soon does mold start in a wet basement?

EPA guidance says mold can start within 24 to 48 hours on wet materials, so drying should start early.

Can I wait until the rain stops?

If water is rising, no. Standing water deepens damage and raises the risk of electrical hazards.
