Mold remediation is the containment, removal and cleaning of mold growth from a building, combined with fixing the moisture source. Mold needs moisture to grow, and the EPA says controlling moisture is the key to controlling mold. A cleaned wall that gets wet again will grow mold again.

If you see growth or smell something musty, avoid disturbing it, since scrubbing can spread spores. Our dispatch line is open 24/7 for La Vista, and a call helps decide whether you need testing, drying or remediation.

Musty smell after a leak: is it mold?

A musty smell after water damage usually means damp materials, and visible mold is a sign the materials stayed wet. Mold often grows behind walls, under carpet and in cabinets where you cannot see it.

Mold can start on wet materials within 24 to 48 hours according to EPA guidance. The sections below explain how remediation is done and how to tell when it is finished.

What remediation under S520 looks like

The ANSI/IICRC S520 standard describes mold remediation practice, and you can ask any company whether its crews follow it. Mold forms after any wet event, so see structural drying and dehumidification and basement water damage cleanup.

1

Fix the moisture

The leak or humidity source is stopped and wet materials are dried first.

2

Contain

Plastic barriers and negative air limit spread to clean rooms.

3

Remove and clean

Porous materials with growth are removed. Hard surfaces are cleaned with HEPA vacuuming and wiping.

4

Verify

The area is inspected and may be tested by an independent party before rebuild.

Health questions belong to a physician. The building work is to dry, remove and clean. If anyone in the home has health concerns, talk with a doctor and stay out of the work area while it is being cleaned.

Testing, clearance and licensing

Testing can help document that a space is clear, but a visual inspection and moisture readings are the starting point. Some buyers and lenders ask for third-party clearance, and an independent tester avoids a conflict of interest.

Ask whether your state licenses mold work, and ask any contractor for their written scope, containment plan and how they handle disposal.

Keeping mold from coming back

The EPA suggests keeping indoor humidity between 30 and 50 percent where possible, and below 60 percent at the most. A hygrometer costs little and shows whether a basement, bathroom or closet is staying too damp.

Run bath and kitchen fans, fix leaks promptly, and keep gutters and downspouts directing water away from the foundation. Dry wet items within a day or two, and replace materials that cannot be dried, such as soaked ceiling tile and carpet pad. These habits matter as much as the cleaning itself.

What shapes the cost of mold remediation

Mold jobs are scoped after an inspection. A written scope and price are explained before work starts.

  • Area affected. More growth means more containment and removal.
  • Materials. Drywall, carpet and insulation are removed, while framing is cleaned.
  • Access. Attics, crawl spaces and tight rooms take longer.
  • Testing. Third-party testing is a separate cost.
  • Moisture source repair. Plumbing or drainage fixes are separate.

The Nebraska Department of Insurance says mold damage is generally not covered by homeowners insurance. Policy wording and any exceptions differ, so check your policy and ask your insurer. If the growth followed a leak in a lower level, the basement water damage cleanup page explains how that space is pumped out and dried first.

Before help arrives, do not brush or scrub a large patch, and keep the HVAC off if you suspect the ducts hold growth.

Mold remediation questions in La Vista

Can I clean mold with bleach?

EPA does not recommend bleach as a routine practice. Fix the moisture, remove porous materials and clean with detergent and water.

How do I know the mold is gone?

Visual inspection, dry readings and sometimes third-party testing. The moisture source must also be fixed.

Does homeowners insurance pay for mold?

Generally not, according to the state insurance department. Policy wording varies, so ask your insurer.

Does Nebraska license mold work?

Ask whether your state licenses mold work and request any required credentials in writing.

Do you answer mold calls at night?

The line is open 24/7, though most mold calls can wait until morning unless water is still spreading.
