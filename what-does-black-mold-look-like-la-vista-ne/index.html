What Does Black Mold Look Like? A La Vista, NE Guide

Black mold is usually dark green, gray or black, but many harmless things look the same. Here is what to look for and what to do next.

Black mold looks like dark green, gray or black spots or patches on a surface, often with a fuzzy, powdery or slimy texture. It usually shows up where moisture lingers, such as bathroom ceilings, window frames, basement walls and behind furniture. A musty smell often comes with it.

Color is a weak clue. The CDC notes that mold can look like spots of many different colors and can smell musty, and that you do not need to know the type before removing it.

Common signs of mold growth

  • Spots or patches in black, gray, green, white or orange
  • Fuzzy, powdery, slimy or speckled texture
  • A musty or earthy smell, even when you cannot see growth
  • Peeling paint or bubbling wallpaper near a damp area
  • Stains that return after wiping
  • Warped drywall, baseboards or ceiling tile

Seeing any of these does not prove mold, but each is a reason to look for moisture.

Where it hides

Mold likes surfaces that stay damp for more than a day or two. Common spots are the underside of carpet pad, the back of drywall behind a leaky fixture, ceiling tile below a roof leak and the walls of an unfinished basement.

Hidden mold is the reason a musty smell with no visible growth deserves attention. If you suspect growth inside a wall, EPA suggests considering an experienced professional.

Look-alikes that are not mold

When in doubt, fix the moisture and clean the surface. If the spot returns or spreads, treat it as mold.

What to do if you think you have mold

  1. Find and stop the water source.
  2. Photograph the area.
  3. Avoid disturbing a large patch, since scrubbing can spread spores.
  4. Dry the area and wet items within 24 to 48 hours when possible.
  5. Decide whether the area is small enough for cleanup, using the EPA's roughly 10 square foot guide.
  6. Call a professional for larger areas, sewage water or suspected hidden growth.

Smell and when to get advice

A musty smell is often the first sign, even before you see anything. The CDC says you should remove mold if you see or smell it, and that you need to clean it up and fix the moisture problem as well.

If anyone in the home has health concerns, talk with a health professional before cleanup. A professional can also tell you whether a patch is small enough for a homeowner or large enough to need containment.

Photograph what you find

Take clear, well-lit photos of the spots from a few feet away and up close, with something for scale such as a coin or a ruler. Note the date, the room and any recent leaks, rain or plumbing work.

Those photos help a professional judge the situation by phone. They also help with an insurance conversation, and they let you see whether the growth is spreading over a week or two.

How to look with a flashlight

Turn off the room light and hold a flashlight at a low angle against the wall. Raised, fuzzy or textured spots catch the light, and flat stains do not.

Check the corners of ceilings, the backs of cabinets, the wall behind a toilet and the baseboard near exterior walls. Do not poke or scrub the area, because disturbing growth can spread spores.

Where La Vista homes tend to show it

La Vista's first homes date to 1959 and 1960, and many have lower levels and window wells. Spots at the base of a basement wall, around a window well or behind stored boxes are common places to check first.

Because the Papillion Creek basin spans about 400 square miles, groundwater and runoff can stay elevated after wet weather. Dampness that lingers in the lowest level of a home near a drainage path is worth monitoring with a hygrometer. Mold identification questions in La Vista

Does black mold always look black?

No. Mold can be many colors, and the CDC says you do not need to know the type before removal.

Where in a La Vista home should I look first?

Check the base of basement walls, around window wells, under sinks and on bathroom ceilings. Those are the places moisture collects.

How can I tell mold from a water stain?

A water stain is usually a flat brown or yellow ring. Mold often has texture, can spread and may smell musty.

Should I have the spots tested?

The CDC does not recommend mold testing and EPA says sampling is usually unnecessary when mold is visible. Fix the moisture and remove the growth.

Is it safe to sleep in a room with visible mold?

That is a question for your doctor. Whatever the answer, find the moisture source and address the growth promptly.
