AC Leak Water Damage Repair in La Vista, NE

Condensate from an air conditioner or furnace coil ends up in ceilings, closets and attics. We trace it, dry it and repair what it touched.

AC leak water damage is the moisture released when an air conditioner's condensate drain clogs, a drain pan rusts through or overflows, or a coil freezes and thaws. An air conditioner pulls water out of the air all season, and when that water has nowhere to go it spills into the ceiling, the closet or the attic floor.

Switch the system off at the thermostat as soon as you see water, because running it keeps producing condensate. Our dispatch line is open 24/7 if the water has already stained a ceiling or is dripping into a room.

Where AC water ends up

Attic air handlers drain through a line to the outdoors or a drain. When that line clogs with algae or debris, the secondary pan fills and the overflow lands on the ceiling below. A closet furnace with an evaporator coil can leak into the floor and the wall base.

The stain often appears days or weeks after the leak starts, because insulation and drywall absorb water before they show it. A ceiling that was dry last month can hold moisture today.

Signs of an AC leak

  • A brown ring or dripping on a ceiling near a vent or air handler location
  • Water in the secondary drain pan, or a pan that is rusted or cracked
  • A musty smell near the closet or attic hatch when the system runs
  • Ice on the evaporator coil or refrigerant lines
  • A float switch that has shut the system off

Any of these is a cue to have the line cleared and the surrounding materials checked with a moisture meter.

Clearing the line is not the end of the job

An HVAC technician clears the condensate line, replaces a damaged pan or fixes a frozen coil. That stops the leak, but it does not dry the insulation, joists and drywall that already absorbed water.

The restoration crew follows with moisture mapping, removal of wet insulation and drying equipment. In La Vista, replacement of HVAC equipment falls under mechanical permits handled by the Building Department, so ask at 402-593-6400 before swapping a unit.

How drying and repair proceed

A ceiling that has stained or sagged is covered in more detail on the ceiling water damage repair page, and the drying approach follows the process on structural drying.

What shapes the cost of AC leak repair

AC leaks are often small, but hidden water can make them larger. A written scope is explained before work starts.

  • Time the leak ran. A long slow leak soaks more insulation.
  • Location. Attic work requires access and protection of ceilings.
  • Materials. Drywall, insulation and flooring below the unit.
  • Drying time. Insulation holds water and can need extended equipment time.
  • HVAC repair. Clearing lines and replacing pans is billed by the HVAC company, separate from restoration.

Water from a sudden equipment failure is sometimes covered, while a leak from poor maintenance often is not. Check your policy and ask your insurer. Water that soaked the ceiling below is covered on the ceiling water damage repair page. Before help arrives, turn the AC off at the thermostat and the breaker, and do not touch wet wiring near the air handler.

AC leak water damage questions in La Vista

Why is my ceiling wet under the attic AC unit?

The most common cause is a clogged condensate line that lets the drain pan overflow. Turn the system off, keep a bucket under the drip and have the line cleared.

Is a musty smell near the air handler a sign of mold?

It can be. A musty smell means damp materials, and mold can follow within 24 to 48 hours of wetting according to EPA guidance. Get moisture readings and have the area dried.

Do you also repair the air conditioner?

No. An HVAC contractor repairs the unit and the drain line. The restoration crew dries and repairs the building materials that got wet.

Can I pour bleach down the condensate line?

Ask your HVAC technician. Many recommend vinegar or manufacturer-approved cleaners, and bleach can damage metal parts.

Do you answer calls about AC leaks at night?

The dispatch line is open 24/7. If water is actively dripping through a ceiling, call and say so.
