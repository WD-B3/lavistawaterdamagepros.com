Water Damage Categories 1, 2 and 3 Explained: La Vista, NE

Category 1 is clean water, Category 2 is gray water and Category 3 is grossly contaminated. The category decides what can be dried and what must go.

Water damage is classified into three categories under the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard: Category 1 is clean water from a sanitary source, Category 2 is significantly contaminated water, and Category 3 is grossly contaminated water that can carry serious contaminants. The category tells a crew how to protect people and what materials can be saved.

Categories are not fixed. Clean water that sits for days, or touches dirty surfaces, can move into a worse category.

The three categories at a glance

Water categories and typical sources
CategoryNameTypical sourcesTypical handling
1Clean waterSupply line break, a leaking faucet, a failed water heaterDry in place when caught early
2Gray waterWashing machine or dishwasher overflow, toilet overflow with no fecesClean and dry, remove porous materials
3Black waterSewage backup, creek or flood water, long-standing waterRemove porous materials, sanitize, use protection

Black water is a term used for Category 3. The water does not need to look black, and the color is not the test.

Category 1: clean water

Category 1 water comes from a sanitary source, such as a supply line, and does not pose a substantial risk at first. It is the most forgiving category because carpet, drywall and wood often have a chance to dry in place.

That window closes with time. After about a day or two, bacteria and materials can change the category, which is another reason to start extraction right away. See water extraction for what the first step looks like.

Category 2: gray water

Gray water has some contamination, so avoid direct contact. Dishwasher and washing machine discharge are typical examples. Porous materials that have soaked up gray water are often removed, and hard surfaces are cleaned and dried.

If you are not sure what you have, treat the water cautiously and keep children and pets away.

Category 3: black water

Category 3 includes sewage, water from a creek or river, and standing water that has been wet for a long time. It can carry bacteria and other contaminants, and crews wear protective equipment.

Porous materials such as carpet, pad and wet drywall are usually removed. Our sewage backup cleanup and flood damage cleanup pages explain how that work is done.

Water looks or smells wrong? Call the line before you touch it.

Why the category matters for your insurance and your health

Category drives scope, so it also drives cost and claim documentation. A sewer backup, for example, is usually covered only with a water backup endorsement, according to the Nebraska Department of Insurance. Flood is not covered under a homeowners policy.

For safety, avoid direct contact with dark, smelly or drain-origin water, and ask a doctor about any exposure.

Staying safe around each category

With Category 1 water, the main risks are slipping and electrical hazards. Keep power off in wet areas, and wear boots and gloves if you are moving items.

With Category 2 and 3 water, avoid skin contact, do not eat or drink in the area, and wash hands after handling anything wet. Keep children and pets out of the affected space until it has been cleaned.

How the category changes the scope

Higher categories mean more material is removed, more cleaning and sanitizing is done and more protective gear is used. Crews may also set up containment so contaminated air does not spread to clean rooms.

That is why a plain-language description matters on the first call. Tell dispatch where the water came from, what it looks like, how it smells and how long it has been there, and we can tell you what to expect.

Category 3 risks near La Vista and Springfield

In the Omaha area the Papillion Creek basin covers about 400 square miles, and storm runoff and creek water both count as Category 3. A flooded lower level in La Vista after a heavy rain is a different water type than a burst pipe on a dry day.

Springfield's floodplain comes from Springfield Creek, and the town's plan records floods in 1903, 1959, 1964 and 1965. For addresses there, assume creek or runoff water is Category 3, and read more on our Springfield, NE page.

Water category questions in La Vista

Is a washing machine overflow in La Vista Category 1 or 2?

Usually Category 2 if it came from the machine's discharge, and Category 1 if it came from the supply hose. A crew checks what the water touched.

Can clean water turn into Category 3?

Yes, if it sits for a long time or contacts contaminants. That is why speed matters.

Is water from a creek near Springfield always Category 3?

Treat it that way. Creek and runoff water can carry contaminants, and porous materials are usually removed.

How do crews tell the category?

By the source, the appearance, the smell and what the water touched. A crew may also ask how long it was there.

Does Category 3 mean the whole house is lost?

No. It means porous materials in contact with the water are removed and hard surfaces are cleaned and sanitized.

Not sure which category? Call and describe the water.
