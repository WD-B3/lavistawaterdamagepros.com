How to Get Rid of Mold in Your La Vista, NE Home

Fix the moisture, protect yourself, clean what can be cleaned, throw away what cannot and dry everything. Here is the sequence and when to call for help.

To get rid of mold in your home, fix the water problem first, then remove the mold and dry everything completely. The EPA says that if you clean up mold but do not fix the moisture, the mold will most likely come back. Small patches on hard surfaces are usually a homeowner job, and larger areas are not.

This guide follows EPA and CDC advice, adapted for La Vista, NE homeowners.

Step 1: find and fix the moisture

Look for leaks at pipes, roofs, windows and appliances, and check humidity in basements and bathrooms. A leak caught early is a drying job. A leak that has been running for weeks may have soaked into drywall and insulation.

If the cause is water that has already spread, the structural drying and dehumidification page explains how wet materials are dried with measured equipment.

Step 2: size the job

How to size a mold job
ObservationSizeNext step

A patch about 3 by 3 feet or smallerAbout 10 square feetOften a homeowner job with protection
Wider growth or repeated returnsLargerCall for assessment
Growth after sewage or flood waterAny sizeCall a professional

Step 3: protect yourself and contain the area

Wear rubber gloves, goggles and a mask suited to dust and spores. Open a window, run a fan that exhausts outdoors and close doors to other rooms. Do not use dry brushing or compressed air, which launch spores into the air.

Do not run the HVAC if you suspect it is contaminated, according to EPA guidance.

Step 4: clean, discard and dry

  1. Scrub hard surfaces with detergent and water.
  2. Rinse or wipe, then dry the surface fully with a fan or dehumidifier.
  3. Throw away porous materials such as ceiling tile and carpet if they are moldy.
  4. Bag waste before carrying it through the house.
  5. Do not paint or caulk over mold, because paint over mold is likely to peel.
  6. Recheck the area for several weeks for any return.

EPA notes that dead mold must still be removed, which is why cleanup is more than killing it.

When to stop and call a professional

  • The area is larger than about 10 square feet
  • The water was sewage or other contaminated water
  • Mold may be in walls, ceilings or the HVAC
  • Anyone in the home has health concerns
  • You cannot find the moisture source

Our mold remediation page describes containment, negative air and clean-up under S520 practice.

What you need for a small job

  • Detergent and water, and clean rags or sponges
  • Rubber gloves, goggles and a suitable mask
  • Heavy trash bags for waste
  • A fan that exhausts outdoors or an open window
  • A dehumidifier to dry the area afterward
  • A hygrometer to track humidity

Skip the dry brush and the compressed air. Both launch spores into the air.

Keeping it from coming back

After cleaning, the goal is a space that stays dry. Fix leaks as soon as you find them, dry wet items within a day or two and keep furniture a few inches from exterior walls so air can move.

In bathrooms, run the fan during and after showers. In basements, keep a dehumidifier running in humid months and watch the hygrometer. If humidity keeps climbing above 60 percent, something else is adding moisture and it needs to be found.

Do not forget the HVAC and the air

EPA says not to run the HVAC system if you know or suspect it is contaminated with mold. Moldy ducts or a wet air handler need a professional look, since running the system spreads spores through the house.

If a musty smell comes through the vents, call for an assessment before using the system heavily.

Damp spots in La Vista, from the basement up

La Vista's original 335 homes were built in 1959 and 1960, and the original homes are now more than 60 years old. Kitchen and bathroom fans that vent outdoors are one of EPA's prevention tips, and they matter most in rooms without good airflow.

If the room is a basement, consider a dehumidifier and a hygrometer. The goal is relative humidity below 60 percent, ideally 30 to 50 percent. Mold cleanup questions in La Vista

What is the first thing to do about mold in my La Vista house?

Find the moisture source and stop it. Cleaning without fixing the water only gives a short break before the mold returns.

Does vinegar kill mold?

Vinegar is sometimes used on hard surfaces, but EPA advises detergent and water and drying completely. Whatever you use, ventilate and fix the moisture.

Can I paint over mold?

No. EPA says paint applied over moldy surfaces is likely to peel, and the mold underneath stays.

How long should I watch for regrowth?

Check the area for several weeks, especially after humid or rainy periods. Regrowth means the moisture is still there.

Do Springfield homes face different mold risks?

Homes near Springfield Creek and runoff paths can stay damp longer after storms. The cleanup steps are the same, and drying remains the priority.
