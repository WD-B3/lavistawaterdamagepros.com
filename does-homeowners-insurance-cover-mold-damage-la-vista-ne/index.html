Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Mold Damage? La Vista, NE

Generally not, according to Nebraska's insurance department, though policy wording varies. Here is how mold is usually treated and how to check your policy.

Homeowners insurance generally does not cover mold damage, according to the Nebraska Department of Insurance's Homeowners Insurance Guide. The same guide says that water damage from negligence, leaks or flooding is generally not covered either. That puts mold in a difficult spot, because mold usually follows water.

Policy wording varies, so this guide cannot say what your policy pays. It explains the common patterns and what to ask.

How mold is commonly treated

Common patterns, not promises
SituationCommon treatmentWhat to ask
Mold from a sudden covered water lossMay be handled as part of that loss, within limitsDoes my policy cap mold coverage?
Mold from a long-term leak or neglectOften excludedHow does my policy define sudden and accidental?
Mold after floodNot covered by homeowners policyDo I have a flood policy?
Mold after sewer backupDepends on water backup endorsementDoes the endorsement include mold?
Mold found at purchaseUsually not coveredWhat did the inspection show?

Some policies add a mold endorsement or a small sublimit. If yours does, the details will be in the policy.

Why prompt drying matters for coverage

If a covered loss happens, the insurer expects you to limit further damage. Drying quickly can keep mold from starting, which protects both the home and the claim. Nebraska's insurance director advises taking temporary measures and keeping receipts for them.

Waiting, or leaving wet materials in place, can turn a covered water loss into a loss with mold that the policy treats differently.

Steps to protect yourself

  1. Call your insurer or agent promptly after a water loss.
  2. Dry the area within 24 to 48 hours when possible.
  3. Photograph damage and keep dated records.
  4. Keep receipts for emergency drying and temporary repairs.
  5. Ask in writing how your policy treats mold and any limit.
  6. Hold off on permanent repairs until the insurer has had a chance to inspect.

What to do if mold is not covered

You can still have it handled. A written scope tells you what is being removed, and fixing the moisture keeps it from returning. Ask whether testing is needed, since the CDC does not recommend testing and the EPA says sampling is usually unnecessary when mold is visible.

If you disagree with an insurer's decision, the Nebraska Department of Insurance lists its phone number as (402) 471-2201.

Reading the policy language

  1. Find the section on exclusions and look for the word mold, fungus or rot.
  2. Look for a sublimit, which is a cap on what the policy pays for mold.
  3. Check how the policy defines sudden and accidental.
  4. See whether a mold or water backup endorsement was added.
  5. Ask your agent in writing how a mold claim would be treated.

These steps take less than an hour and can save a lot of confusion later.

What to keep for a claim

  • Dated photos of the damage and the source of the water
  • Receipts for drying and temporary repairs
  • Reports or readings from any professional visit
  • Notes of calls with your insurer and agent
  • Copies of any estimates or scopes of work

The Nebraska Director of Insurance has advised keeping copies of expenses relating to temporary repairs and notes of phone calls with the insurer.

Renters and landlords

If you rent, your renters policy covers your belongings and not the building. Tell your landlord promptly about any leak or growth, in writing, and keep a copy.

A landlord's policy covers the structure under its own terms. Responsibility for repairs depends on your lease and local law, so ask both parties and keep records.

What insurers often ask for

  • When you first noticed the problem and what you did
  • The cause of the water, if known
  • Photos and videos of the damage
  • Receipts and invoices for work done
  • Records of past maintenance, such as plumbing repairs

Answer plainly and keep copies of everything you send.

If your claim is denied

Ask for the reason in writing and compare it with the policy language. If you disagree, you can contact the Nebraska Department of Insurance, whose consumer line is listed on its homeowners guide.

Keep drying and cleaning in the meantime. Delay makes damage worse, and records of what you did are valuable whatever happens with the claim.

Mold, coverage and La Vista homes

La Vista's first 335 homes were built in 1959 and 1960. In houses of that age, original plumbing, window wells and basement walls are common places where leaks start, and mold claims often hinge on whether a leak was sudden or long-term.

The Papillion Creek basin covers about 400 square miles, and flood coverage is a separate policy. If a creek-related flood leads to mold, the homeowners policy generally does not cover the flood, so a separate flood policy matters. Mold and insurance questions in La Vista

Will my La Vista homeowners policy pay for mold removal?

Generally not, according to the Nebraska Department of Insurance. Your policy may have an exception or a limit, so ask your insurer.

Is mold covered if it follows a burst pipe?

Sometimes it is treated as part of that covered loss, within limits. Ask your insurer and keep dated records.

Does flood insurance cover mold?

That depends on the flood policy and the situation. Ask the flood insurer how they treat mold.

Do I need a test to file a mold claim?

Ask your insurer what they require. The CDC does not recommend testing, and EPA says sampling is usually unnecessary when mold is visible.

What if I rent in La Vista?

A tenant's renters policy and a landlord's policy are different, so ask both and tell your landlord promptly about moisture.
