Slab leak water damage is the moisture that comes from a pressurized water line running under or inside a concrete foundation slab. The leak is hidden, so the first sign is often a symptom elsewhere in the house.

Warm spots, running-water sounds and a higher water bill: is it a slab leak?

A slab leak usually announces itself in three ways: a warm patch on the floor above a hot water line, the sound of water running when every fixture is off, or a water bill that jumps with no change in use. Some leaks also show as damp carpet edges or a musty smell at the base of walls.

If you notice any of these, shut off the main and check the meter. The next section explains how the problem is confirmed and how the structure is dried.

Water coming through the floor: emergency steps

Stop the supply first. Then protect the materials that are wicking water.

1Close the main valve

This stops the supply to the slab line.

2Check the meter

With everything off, a moving meter dial suggests a leak. Ask MUD at 402-554-6666 if you need help reading the meter.

3Stay out of live circuits

If water reaches an outlet or the panel, call the utility or 911 before calling us.

4Lift and fan

Move furniture and rugs off the wet area.

5Do not drill

Do not break the slab yourself. Leak detection tools locate the line without guessing.

6Call dispatch

Say where the water appears and whether the floor is warm.

Is your home built on a slab?

Whether a slab sits under your home or only under a garage or addition is a fact about your house, not a guess about La Vista. Your closing documents or inspection report usually state the foundation type, and a plumber can confirm quickly.

In a home with a basement, a leak in a line running below the lowest floor behaves like a slab leak. The restoration steps are the same, though access to the line differs.

How the leak is found and the damage dried

The drying stage follows the process in structural drying and dehumidification. Wet carpet may need carpet restoration and wet wood flooring may need hardwood floor repair.

1

Pressure and meter test

A plumber isolates the line and listens or uses leak detection equipment.

2

Repair or reroute

The line is repaired, or replaced by a new route overhead.

3

Extract and dry

The restoration crew removes standing water and dries flooring and wall bases.

4

Verify

Moisture readings confirm the floor and walls are dry before rebuild.

What shapes the cost of slab leak cleanup

The plumbing repair and the restoration are separate costs. A written restoration scope is explained before work starts.

  • Duration of the leak. A leak that ran for weeks has soaked more materials.
  • Flooring above. Carpet, hardwood, tile and vinyl dry or fail differently.
  • Rooms affected. Water travels to adjacent wall bases.
  • Drying time. Slab moisture dries slowly.
  • Plumbing repair. Locating and fixing the line is a plumber's cost.

Insurance policies often treat a sudden break differently from a gradual leak, and some exclude access to the line. Ask your insurer exactly what is covered, and keep the plumber's report. The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard guides how the resulting wet materials are dried, while the pipe repair itself belongs to a plumber. Before help arrives, shut off the main water and do not run appliances that draw water.

Slab leak questions in La Vista

Do you handle slab leak water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in La Vista?

Yes. Dispatch runs 24/7. Close the main valve, call, and tell us where water is showing.

How do you know if you have a slab leak?

Common signs are a warm spot on the floor, the sound of running water with everything off and an unexplained jump in the water bill. A plumber confirms it with testing.

Do you fix the leaking pipe?

No. A plumber locates and repairs the line. We dry the structure and prepare it for rebuild.

Will the slab need to be broken open?

Sometimes, but often the line can be rerouted through walls or ceilings instead. That is a plumber's decision.

Does homeowners insurance cover slab leaks?

Coverage varies. Some policies cover resulting water damage but not the cost of reaching the pipe. Ask your insurer before work starts.
