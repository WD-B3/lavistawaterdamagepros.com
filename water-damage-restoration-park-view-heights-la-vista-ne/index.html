Park View Heights is an established residential area of La Vista. The name is used on several plats, including a 2nd Addition, and a neighborhood elementary school carries it. Central Park, which the city created when the La Vista Falls golf course was redesigned, is at 8305 Park View Boulevard on the east side of 84th Street.

We describe this area approximately because we did not confirm official boundaries. If you are unsure whether your address is covered, call and ask.

Split-entry homes and daylight basements

A listing for a 1971 home on South Glenview Drive in Park View Heights describes a split-entry house with a partially finished daylight basement and a walkout. That is one example, and we are not saying every home here matches it. It does show a common older layout, with a lower level that sits partly above grade.

A half-buried lower level meets the ground along its back wall, so water can seep in at a foundation crack or a window well after heavy rain. A leak from the kitchen or bath above can drip into the same space. See basement water damage cleanup and kitchen water damage restoration for how each is handled.

Safe first steps

Keep the first hour simple and avoid anything that puts you in contact with water and electricity.

1Stop the source

Close the fixture valve or the main valve if it is dry and within reach.

2Leave electrical alone

Do not touch breakers or outlets while standing in or near water.

3Keep out of dark water

Water from a drain or toilet should be treated as contaminated.

4Lift and move

Raise furniture legs onto blocks and move papers and electronics up.

5Photograph first

Take wide and close photos of the waterline and the source.

6Call dispatch

Describe what is wet and ask what to do next.
