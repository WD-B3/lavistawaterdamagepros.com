Moisture mapping, air movers and dehumidifiers dry framing, subfloor and drywall to a verified dry reference.

Structural drying and dehumidification is the measured removal of moisture from framing, subfloors, drywall and contents after the free water has been extracted. It is the stage that prevents mold and warping, and it is the core of the water mitigation process.

Our dispatch line is open 24/7 if water is still spreading. If the water is already out, call to schedule moisture mapping and a drying plan with written readings.

How long does it take to dry out a wet house?

Most structures take several days of equipment time rather than hours, and the real answer depends on the materials, the amount of water and the humidity in the house. Drying is finished when moisture readings match a dry reference, not when the air feels comfortable.

The next sections explain what is measured, what equipment is used and how the crew decides the job is done.

Moisture mapping and the drying plan

The crew starts with moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera to find where water traveled. Readings are recorded on a map and repeated daily. A dry reference is taken from an unaffected wall or floor.

A hygrometer records temperature and humidity so the crew can see whether the dehumidifiers are pulling moisture from the air and not just moving it around.

What gets dried and what gets removed

Not every wet material can be saved, and the crew decides by reading, not by sight. Solid framing, plywood subfloor and most concrete dry well. Fiberglass insulation, carpet pad and soft drywall that stayed wet usually come out, because they hold water and are cheaper to replace than to dry.

Drywall is the usual judgment call. If it is clean water and the board is not swollen or sagging, it can often be dried in place with controlled airflow. If it is crumbling, stained from contaminated water or has been wet for days, cutting it out is the safer path.

What to expect while the equipment runs

Drying equipment is loud and uses real electricity, so ask the crew how many circuits it needs and avoid plugging other heavy appliances into the same line. Keep windows and doors closed in the drying area so the dehumidifiers work against a limited volume of air.

You will see the crew return each day to check readings and move equipment. That monitoring is part of the process, and the written log is what proves the structure reached a dry reference.

Why speed and measurement matter

EPA guidance says wet materials should be dried within 24 to 48 hours to limit mold. Drywall and wood keep absorbing water and holding it against framing, so the sooner equipment is set, the fewer materials need to be torn out.

Measurement prevents two errors. Stopping too early leaves wet framing behind finished walls. Running equipment too long wastes time and power.

What shapes the cost of structural drying

Drying is billed on equipment and monitoring. A written scope and price are explained before work starts.

  • Affected area. More square footage means more air movers and dehumidifiers.
  • Materials. Hardwood, plaster and framing dry slowly.
  • Water category. Contaminated water adds removal steps.
  • Days of equipment. The job continues until readings are dry.
  • Access. Walls and floors may need to be opened for airflow.

Insurers often require documentation of daily readings. Ask the crew for a copy of the moisture map and keep it with your claim. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard behind this method, and it centers on measured moisture readings and dry reference points. While drying is underway, do not switch equipment off, and do not use cords or fans near standing water.

Drying questions in La Vista

How long does it take to dry out a wet house?

Several days is common, but it depends on the materials, the water and the humidity. The job ends when moisture readings match a dry reference.

Can I just run fans and a household dehumidifier?

For a small spill on a hard floor, maybe. Larger losses need commercial air movers and LGR dehumidifiers and measured readings.

Why do you take readings every day?

Readings show whether drying is working. They let the crew move or remove equipment and prove the structure is dry.

Will drying cause damage to hardwood?

Fast drying can cup or crack wood, which is why wood floors are dried with controlled airflow and monitored.

Do you provide drying logs for insurance?

Ask for the moisture map and daily readings. Insurers often want them.
