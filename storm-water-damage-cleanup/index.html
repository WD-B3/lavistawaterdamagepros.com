Storm water damage is the interior damage from wind, hail or heavy rain that enters through a broken envelope: shingles, flashing, windows, siding or doors. It is not rising floodwater, which is covered separately.

After the storm: what to do first

Safety first, then limit further water entry, then document.

1Check for hazards

Stay clear of downed lines, broken glass and unstable trees.

2Stop more water

Cover openings only if you can do so safely. Keep receipts for temporary measures.

3Protect belongings

Move items away from leaks. Pull rugs off wet floors.

4Photograph everything

Capture roof damage from the ground and all wet areas inside.

5Call your insurer

The Nebraska Department of Insurance advises contacting your insurer as soon as you can after storm damage.

6Call dispatch

How storm water gets inside

Wind lifts shingles and drives rain under flashing. Hail bruises roofing and can crack skylights and siding. Heavy rain overwhelms gutters and finds gaps around windows. Each path soaks different materials: attic insulation and ceilings for roofs, wall cavities for windows and siding.

The water usually enters in one place and shows up in another. A ceiling stain two rooms from the roof damage is common, which is why moisture mapping follows the path instead of trusting the stain.

Working with your insurer after a storm

The Nebraska Department of Insurance advises homeowners to contact the insurer promptly, take temporary measures to reduce further damage, keep receipts and hold off on permanent repairs until the insurer has inspected. Do not throw away damaged items before the adjuster sees them.

The state also advises knowing who you are dealing with before agreeing to repairs. Get references, written estimates and proof of insurance, and remember that a residential contract may be canceled within three days if the insurer later denies the claim.

Ask your agent whether wind and hail damage carries a separate deductible, since policies can treat those perils differently.

What shapes the cost of storm water cleanup

Storm losses vary with the size of the opening and how long water entered. A written scope and price are explained before work starts.

  • Number of openings. Roof, window and siding damage each let water into different areas.
  • Time before protection. Longer exposure soaks more materials.
  • Interior finishes. Ceilings, insulation, walls and flooring.
  • Drying days. Attics and cavities dry slowly.
  • Roof and exterior repair. Handled by a roofer, separate from restoration.

Storm damage is often covered, but flood damage is usually excluded and wear and tear is not covered. Check your policy and keep photos and receipts. Water that comes through the roof into the ceiling is covered on ceiling water damage repair, and a wet lower level is on basement water damage cleanup.

Storm water damage questions in La Vista

Do you handle storm water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in La Vista?

Yes. Dispatch runs 24/7. If you cannot reach the roof safely, call and describe what you see from the ground.

Should I tarp the roof myself?

Only if it is dry, calm and safe. Roof work in wet or windy conditions causes serious injuries. A roofer can tarp safely.

Should I file a claim for a small leak?

It depends on the damage and your deductible. Ask your insurer before deciding, and photograph the damage either way.

Do you repair roofs?

No. A roofer repairs the roof. We dry and restore the interior.

Can I wait on drying until the roofer arrives?

It is better not to. Wet insulation and drywall can start growing mold within 24 to 48 hours, according to EPA guidance.
