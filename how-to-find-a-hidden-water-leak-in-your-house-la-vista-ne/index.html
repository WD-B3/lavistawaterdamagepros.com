How to Find a Hidden Water Leak in Your La Vista, NE House

A hidden leak shows up in your water bill, your meter, your walls and your nose. Here are simple checks you can do before you call anyone.

To find a hidden water leak, look for changes you cannot explain: a higher water bill, a meter that moves when nothing is running, a musty smell, stains on a ceiling or wall, or a floor that feels soft or warm. Then check the usual suspects, toilets, supply lines and outdoor spigots, before opening walls.

Many leaks are small and slow, and they are cheap to fix compared with the damage they cause over months.

Signs that suggest a hidden leak

  • A water bill that rises without a change in use
  • Sound of running water when everything is off
  • Stains, bubbling paint or peeling wallpaper
  • Warped baseboards or soft floor areas
  • A musty smell in one room
  • Mold growth in an unusual place
  • Wet spots on a ceiling below a bathroom

Any one of these deserves a closer look. Two or more usually mean a leak.

Simple checks you can do yourself

1

Meter test

Turn off all water use, find your meter, and watch the small leak indicator or dial for movement over a few minutes. Movement suggests a leak after the meter.

2

Toilet dye test

MUD suggests adding food coloring to the tank and waiting 15 minutes. Color in the bowl means a tank-to-bowl leak, which can waste about a quart per flush.

3

Faucets and hoses

Check under sinks, at washing machine hoses and at outdoor spigots. MUD notes a hose left running uses nearly 300 gallons an hour.

4

Walls and ceilings

Look for new stains, and feel for cool or damp spots with the back of your hand.

When the leak is behind a wall or under a slab

Hidden leaks behind walls or under floors need tools. Moisture meters and thermal imaging show cooler or wetter areas behind finished surfaces without cutting open the wall. A plumber can pressure-test lines or use acoustic tools to locate a leak.

Our structural drying page explains how wet areas are mapped, and the slab leak page covers lines under concrete. A crew dries the damage, and a plumber fixes the pipe.

What to do once you find it

  1. Shut off the water at the fixture or the main valve.
  2. Photograph the area and any damage.
  3. Dry wet materials within 24 to 48 hours when possible.
  4. Call a plumber to repair the leak.
  5. Call your insurer if the damage is significant, since most policies expect you to limit further damage.
  6. Do not repaint or close up a wall until it is dry.

If a pipe has burst and water is spreading, see burst pipe water damage.

Outdoor lines and irrigation

A leak between the meter and the house can run for weeks without showing indoors. Look for a patch of lawn that stays soggy, a sprinkler zone that never dries or a meter that moves when everything inside is off.

Because the leak is on your side of the meter, repair is your responsibility. A plumber or irrigation contractor can locate and fix it.

Questions to ask a plumber

  1. How will you locate the leak without cutting open more than needed?
  2. Can you show me the pressure test or the readings?
  3. Is the line worth repairing or should it be replaced?
  4. Will I need a permit for this work?
  5. What should I do about the wet area while you work?

Ask the plumber to tell your restoration crew where the leak was, so the drying plan covers the right area.

The cost of waiting

A small leak that runs for weeks can soak drywall, insulation and subfloor, and it can lead to mold. Fixing it early is almost always cheaper than repairing the damage later.

If the leak has already damaged materials, dry them within 24 to 48 hours when you can and keep the records.

Water service and who to call in La Vista

Water service in the metro area comes from the Metropolitan Utilities District. MUD's customer service line is 402.554.6666 on weekdays, and its emergency hotline for water main breaks is 402.554.7777, open 24/7. A break on the utility's side of the meter is theirs, and a leak after the meter is yours.

La Vista's housing dates to 1959, so original supply lines and shutoff valves may be old. The City of La Vista Building Department at 402-593-6400 handles plumbing permits for replacements. Calls from Springfield follow the same steps, with local details on the Springfield, NE page and the full coverage list on the service area page. These checks follow the Metropolitan Utilities District's own conservation guidelines for finding household leaks.

Hidden leak questions in La Vista

Who do I call if the leak is outside my La Vista house at the street?

MUD's emergency hotline is 402.554.7777 for water main breaks. A leak after your meter is your responsibility.

Can I find a leak under a slab myself?

Not reliably. Signs include warm floor spots, a moving meter with everything off and the sound of running water. A plumber uses tools to locate it.

How do I check my water meter for a leak?

Turn off all water use, find the meter and watch its small indicator for several minutes. If it moves, water is flowing somewhere.

Does a leak under a sink need a claim?

Often a repair and drying are enough. Check the size of the loss against your deductible first.

What if the leak is in a Springfield home?

The steps are the same, but check with the city office about who supplies your water and who to call for a main break.
