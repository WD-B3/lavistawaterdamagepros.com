Water damage restoration in Springfield, NE covers the removal, drying and repair work that follows a leak, a storm or a rising creek. Springfield sits in southwestern Sarpy County, and our dispatch line for it is open 24/7 from the La Vista base.

If water is moving through a Springfield house right now, shut off the main valve, keep power off in wet rooms unless the panel is dry, and call. Everything else on this page explains what happens after that call.

When water is spreading in Springfield

Small towns do not always have a plumber on call at midnight, so the first hour is often yours. These cards cover what to do before a crew arrives.

1Close the main valve

Find where the water line enters the house and turn the valve clockwise. A frozen or stuck valve is worth a call to the city office.

2Kill power to wet areas

Switch off breakers for wet rooms only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without standing in water.

3Lift and move

Raise furniture legs onto blocks, pull rugs, and carry electronics and papers to a dry floor.

4Avoid dark water

Water that smells, looks dark or came from a drain can carry contamination. Keep kids and pets out.

5Photograph first

Take wide shots and close shots of the waterline before anything is removed.

6Call the 24/7 line

Give the address, the source and how deep the water is. Ask what to do until the crew arrives.

Springfield Creek and the flood of June 1964

Springfield's floodplain comes from Springfield Creek, and the town's own long-range plan lists recorded floods in 1903, 1959, 1964 and 1965. The largest was on June 16 and 17, 1964, which overtopped State Highway 50, several residences and the Sarpy County Fairgrounds.

That history matters for how a call is handled. Water entering from a creek is not clean supply water, so it follows the contamination rules described on the flood damage cleanup page. Anyone with a lower level near the creek should ask which mapped zone their lot falls in.

Where Springfield's runoff goes

The city and its surrounding area lie in the Lower Platte watershed. Turtle Creek and Buffalo Creek drain toward the Platte River, and Springfield Creek and Turtle Creek carry runoff to the town itself.

After a heavy rain, that runoff can pool at window wells and foundation walls. The result is a wet lower level, which is handled on the basement water damage cleanup page, or a flooded yard that sends water toward a garage.

Roads, utilities and who handles what

State Highway 50, also known as 144th Street, runs north and south through the Springfield area, and Highway 370 and Platteview Road carry east and west traffic. Interstate 80 is nearby. When a creek overtops a road, crews may need to plan another route, so give dispatch your cross streets.

Electric service comes from the Omaha Public Power District, according to the town's plan. Water and gas for the Highway 370 and 144th Street commercial area come from the Metropolitan Utilities District, and sewer there connects to Omaha's Papio treatment plant. Ask the City of Springfield office who serves your street and which permits apply, since La Vista's rules are not Springfield's.

ZIP code 68059 and nearby streets

Springfield uses ZIP code 68059. We serve every address in 68059, from the town center near Springfield Creek out to the roads that lead to Highway 370 and Platteview Road. Our home base is La Vista, ZIP 68128, and the same crews work both areas.

Springfield was platted in 1873 by J. D. Spearman, a Civil War veteran, ahead of the Missouri Pacific Railroad, and was named for the springs nearby. In 2020 the census counted 1,501 people. The MoPac Trail runs south toward the Platte River, and the annual Sarpy County Fair is held in town each August.

What shapes the cost of water damage in Springfield

Springfield jobs are scoped the same way as any other. A written scope and price are explained before work starts.

  • Source of the water. Creek water and supply water follow different procedures.
  • Area affected. More rooms and more square feet need more equipment.
  • Time on site. Dry-out days depend on how deep the water went.
  • Materials. Carpet, drywall and flooring each need their own steps.
  • Distance. Travel from the La Vista base is part of planning the visit.

Insurance usually treats sudden pipe or appliance failure differently from flood. Ask your insurer, and photograph the loss before cleanup.
