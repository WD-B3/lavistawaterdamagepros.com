How to Get Rid of Black Mold: La Vista, NE Homeowner Guide

You get rid of black mold the same way you get rid of other mold: fix the moisture, then remove and clean. Here is the sequence and the limits of DIY.

Getting rid of black mold starts with the moisture that fed it, because cleaning mold from a wall that keeps getting wet only works until the next leak. The EPA says plainly that the key to mold control is moisture control. After the water problem is fixed, the mold is removed and the area is cleaned and dried.

The label black mold is popular, but color does not tell you the species or the risk. The CDC says you do not need to know the type of mold before removing it.

Step 1: fix the water first

Find the leak, the condensation or the humidity source and stop it. EPA guidance says to dry water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours when you can, and to fix plumbing leaks as soon as possible. If the cause is a wet basement, a wet wall or an old leak, see our structural drying page.

Indoor humidity matters too. EPA recommends keeping it below 60 percent, ideally between 30 and 50 percent, and a hygrometer is a cheap way to check.

Step 2: decide whether you can do it yourself

The EPA's less than about 10 square feet rule means roughly a three by three foot patch.

Step 3: clean hard surfaces and discard what cannot be cleaned

On hard surfaces such as tile or sealed wood, scrub with detergent and water and dry completely. The CDC says a bleach solution of no more than 1 cup of household bleach in 1 gallon of water can be used, and never mix bleach with ammonia or other cleaners. EPA does not recommend bleach as a routine practice in mold cleanup.

Ceiling tile, carpet and other absorbent items that grow mold are often discarded, since the growth is hard to get out of them. Hold off on paint and caulk until the surface is clean and dry, because a coat over mold tends to flake off.

Protect yourself while you work

  • Open windows or use a fan that exhausts outdoors
  • Gloves, eye protection and a dust mask suited to spores
  • Keep children, pets and anyone with health concerns away
  • Keep the furnace and air conditioner off if you suspect the ducts hold growth
  • Talk to a health professional first if you have health concerns

If you see mold across a wide area, smell it without finding it or the water came from a sewer, call for help. Our mold remediation page explains containment, removal and clearance.

After the cleanup, confirm it worked

Recheck the area every few days for the first few weeks, and again after the next humid stretch or heavy rain. Mold that returns in the same spot tells you the moisture source is still active, and cleaning again will not fix that.

Keep humidity in range, run bathroom and kitchen fans that vent outdoors, and fix new leaks fast. If you hired a company, ask what was removed, what readings were taken and what they recommend monitoring.

Common mistakes with black mold

  • Painting over mold without removing it
  • Using bleach on porous materials and expecting it to reach below the surface
  • Mixing bleach with ammonia or other cleaners
  • Dry-scrubbing or sweeping a large patch
  • Skipping the moisture fix because the spot looks gone

When mold is a health question first

If anyone in the home has health concerns, talk to a doctor before cleanup starts, as EPA advises.

Consider having someone else do the work, or moving the person to another room until the area is cleaned and dry.

La Vista basements and the dampness that feeds mold

La Vista was planned in 1959 on land overlooking the Big Papillion Creek basin, and the Papillion Creek watershed covers about 400 square miles. After wet periods, groundwater can push on foundation walls, and a basement that stays damp is where black-looking growth tends to show up first.

For a quick check, run a hygrometer for a few days in the lowest level and compare it with the EPA range. The City of La Vista Building Department at 402-593-6400 can tell you whether repairs to drainage or plumbing need a permit. When the dampness comes from a lower level, the basement water damage cleanup page explains the pumping and drying.

Black mold questions in La Vista

Is black mold in a La Vista basement more dangerous than other mold?

Color alone does not tell you the species. Removal and moisture control are the same either way, according to CDC and EPA guidance.

Can I use bleach on black mold?

On hard surfaces the CDC lists a weak bleach mix with fresh air, while EPA treats bleach as a judgment call, not a standard step. Never combine it with ammonia.

Should I test for black mold?

The CDC does not recommend mold testing, and EPA says sampling is usually unnecessary if mold is visible. Focus on removal and moisture control.

When is black mold a professional job?

When the area is larger than about 10 square feet, the water was sewage, the HVAC may be contaminated or you suspect hidden growth.

Does my insurance cover black mold removal?

Mold damage is generally not covered under homeowners policies according to the Nebraska Department of Insurance, though wording varies, so ask your insurer.
