Flood water is an emergency from the first inch, and La Vista dispatch answers 24/7. Stay out of dark or moving water, turn off power only if the panel is dry and reachable, lift valuables and keep children and pets away. If water is near outlets, a furnace or the panel, call the utility or 911 before you call us.

Flood damage restoration covers water that enters from outside, such as creek overflow, surface runoff or groundwater. Under ANSI/IICRC S500 practice, that water is treated as contaminated, so cleanup includes removal of porous materials, sanitizing and drying, not just pumping.

When flood water is an emergency

Flood water can carry silt, chemicals and sewage. These steps keep people safe while a crew is on the way.

1Get out of the water

Do not wade if the water is dark, deep, moving or touching electrical equipment.

2Do not use appliances

Leave washers, furnaces and electronics alone until they are inspected.

3Shut off gas if you smell it

Leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 from outside.

4Save only safe items

Lift dry valuables and documents. Do not salvage food that touched flood water.

5Photograph the waterline

Capture each room and any marks on the walls before cleanup.

6Call dispatch

Tell us the depth, the source and whether the water smells.

The Big Papillion Creek basin and La Vista

La Vista's name comes from the view over the Big Papillion Creek basin, and the city's earliest homes were built on a site overlooking the creek floodplain. The Papillion Creek watershed covers about 400 square miles across Washington, Douglas and Sarpy counties, and a third of Nebraska's population lives in it, according to coverage of the 2019 flood response.

State officials put losses from the spring 2019 flooding at nearly $1.4 billion statewide. What shapes a flood cleanup price

Flood work is scoped after a site visit because every loss is different. A written scope and price are explained before work starts.

  • Water category. Contaminated flood water adds removal and sanitizing steps.
  • Depth and duration. Longer exposure pushes water into framing and finishes.
  • Square footage. More floor area means more equipment and labor.
  • Materials removed. Carpet, pad, insulation and drywall are removed and rebuilt later.
  • Drying time. Wet lower levels can need extended equipment time.

Flood damage is usually excluded from homeowners policies and needs separate flood coverage, according to the Nebraska Department of Insurance. In October 2026 the state's insurance director said only about 5 percent of Americans carry flood insurance. Check your policy or ask your insurer. Water that reaches a lower level is covered on the basement water damage cleanup page, and the mold remediation page applies if materials stay wet.

Flood damage questions in La Vista

Do you handle flood damage emergencies at night or on weekends in La Vista?

Yes. Dispatch is open 24/7, and crews respond the same day when available. If water is still rising, move to higher ground first and call from there.

Is flood water considered contaminated?

Yes. Under S500 practice flood water is treated as contaminated because it picks up soil, runoff and sewage. That changes what can be kept and how surfaces are cleaned.

Does homeowners insurance cover a flood?

Usually not. The Nebraska Department of Insurance says flooding is generally not covered by a standard homeowners policy and directs people to a separate flood policy. Ask your agent.

How do I know if my home is in a floodplain?

Use the FEMA Flood Map Service Center or the Papio-Missouri River Natural Resources District's online viewers. Check the FEMA flood map for your address.

How soon do I need to start drying?

As soon as it is safe. EPA guidance says wet materials should dry within 24 to 48 hours to limit mold, so call early.
