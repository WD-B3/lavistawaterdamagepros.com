Should You File a Claim for a Small Water Leak? La Vista, NE

It depends on the size of the loss compared with your deductible, and on what a claim could do to your future premiums. Here is how to weigh it.

Whether to file a claim for a small water leak depends on three things: how big the loss really is, how it compares with your deductible and what a claim could mean for your premiums. A small, visible stain may be the tip of a larger wet area. A claim is worth thinking through, but it is not always the right answer.

The Nebraska Department of Insurance says claims history for your home and nearby homes is a factor in premiums, and that small claims can cost more in the long run. Those lines are worth reading before you decide.

Find out how big the leak really is

Surface stains underestimate water damage. A moisture meter and thermal imaging can show whether the wet area is a few inches or a few feet. Ask for readings before you decide, and photograph the area first.

If the leak is still active, stop it and take temporary measures, since most policies expect you to limit further damage.

A way to weigh the decision

Questions that help decide whether to claim
QuestionLeans toward filingLeans toward not filing
Loss compared with deductibleWell above the deductibleNear or below the deductible
Spread of the damageBehind walls, under floors or in several roomsOne small, dry, visible area
Water typeGray or contaminated waterClean water from a short leak
CauseSudden and accidentalSlow leak or wear
Claim historyFew or no claimsSeveral recent claims

If you are unsure, you can call your insurer or agent and ask general questions before deciding to file.

What can happen if you file

The state guide says insurers track claims and use them for risk assessment, and that claims-free homes may qualify for discounts. Some insurers may adjust rates or renewal terms after claims. That does not mean you should avoid a claim you need, only that you should know the tradeoff.

Keep in mind that policies have exclusions. Mold, flood and long-term leaks are often excluded, and the water backup coverage is a separate endorsement.

If you decide not to file

You can still have the area dried and repaired. Prompt drying limits the loss, and prices are explained before work starts. See the water extraction page and the structural drying and dehumidification page for what that includes.

Keep photos, receipts and readings anyway. If the damage turns out to be larger than expected, you will have a record.

A worked example with made-up numbers

Suppose a washing machine hose leaks and the cleanup and repair total $1,500. If your deductible is $1,000, the most a covered claim could pay is about $500, before any other policy terms. After a claim, that loss may also appear on your record.

Now suppose the same hose leak soaked a wall and floor, and the total is $9,000. The same deductible would leave about $8,000 for the insurer to consider. The numbers here are only an illustration, and your policy and your insurer decide the real result.

Document the leak whether or not you file

  • Date and time you found it, and what you did to stop it
  • Photos and video before and after cleanup
  • The failed part, if you can keep it
  • Receipts for drying, repairs and supplies
  • Moisture readings from any professional visit

Those records cost nothing to keep, and they matter if damage shows up later or if the house is sold.

Questions to ask your agent before you file

  1. How does my deductible apply to this kind of loss?
  2. Does the policy treat this as sudden and accidental?
  3. How are claims reported, and does an inquiry count as a claim?
  4. How do claims affect renewals or discounts?
  5. Are there limits for water, mold or sewer backup?
  6. What documents will you want to see?

Ask these in general terms first. You can usually get answers without filing anything, and then decide.

A small leak in a La Vista house

La Vista's first homes date to 1959 and 1960. In houses that old, small leaks often start at original supply lines, water heaters and washing machine hoses, and the City of La Vista Building Department at 402-593-6400 can answer questions about plumbing permits for replacements.

The Metropolitan Utilities District suggests simple home checks, including a food coloring test in the toilet tank, where color appearing in the bowl after 15 minutes shows a leak that can waste about a quart of water with each flush. That kind of leak rarely justifies a claim on its own, but it is worth fixing. Wet drywall near the leak is covered on the wall and drywall repair page.

Small leak and claim questions in La Vista

Will one small claim raise my La Vista home insurance rates?

It may, and policies differ. Nebraska's insurance department says claims history for your home and nearby homes is a premium factor, so ask your agent how your insurer treats it.

What if the leak is smaller than my deductible?

Then a claim likely will not pay anything. You can still dry and repair it yourself or hire help.

Should I fix the leak before the adjuster comes?

Stop the water and take temporary measures. Hold permanent repairs until your insurer has had a chance to inspect, as Nebraska's insurance director advises.

How can I tell if a toilet is leaking?

MUD suggests adding food coloring to the tank and waiting 15 minutes. Color in the bowl means a tank-to-bowl leak.

Can you help document a small loss?

Yes. We take moisture readings and photographs that you can share with your insurer, whether or not you decide to file.
